Two small models, one gold price

Chronos-2 and TimesFM are not language models with a finance hat on. They are forecasters — small, pretrained, and specifically designed to run without ever seeing your data before.

What these models actually are

time-series foundation models — pretrained forecasters

Both are transformers pretrained on very large collections of time series — energy demand, retail sales, web traffic, weather, finance — so that they learn the general shapes sequences take. You then hand one a series it has never seen and it forecasts. No training, no fitting, no gradients. That property is called zero-shot, and it is the whole reason this POC is cheap.

Why they run on your laptop

four reasons, and none of them is "we got lucky"

The instinct that a "foundation model" needs a datacentre comes from language models. That intuition does not transfer, for four separate reasons:

A useful LLMThese models
Size7 B – 500 B params120 M – 200 M
GenerationOne token at a time, thousands of passesThe whole forecast in one pass
Vocabulary~100,000 tokensA few thousand buckets, or none at all
Your useOften fine-tuned — gradients, optimiser state, ~4× the weights in RAMZero-shot — forward passes only

A 120M-parameter forward pass over a 60-step window is arithmetic your CPU finishes in milliseconds. The 1.34 GB of weights sits in RAM once and is reused. Nothing is being learned, so there is no optimiser state, no backward pass, and no training data resident.

And the slow part isn't slow either. Evaluating 145 cut-off dates is not 145 sequential forecasts — it is 145 independent context windows, which both models take as a single batch. That is why a 2017 mobile CPU is enough: the workload is wide, not deep.

Why buckets rather than a price

"Gold will be 10,412.30 tomorrow" is unfalsifiable in any useful sense — it is wrong by some amount, always. Instead the forecast is a probability across five bands defined in this instrument's own volatility:

BucketConditionGold, t+1, today
large downr < −1.5σbelow −2.76%
small down−1.5σ ≤ r < −0.5σ−2.76% … −0.93%
flat−0.5σ ≤ r < 0.5σ−0.93% … +0.94%
small up0.5σ ≤ r < 1.5σ+0.94% … +2.84%
large upr ≥ 1.5σabove +2.84%

Scoring uses ranked probability score, which knows the buckets are ordered: predicting "large up" when the answer was "small up" costs less than predicting "large down". Plain accuracy would treat those two mistakes as identical.

The five things being compared

all scored on identical days, or the comparison means nothing

RungWhat it seesWhy it's there

timesfm · covGold + 10 covariatesSame question for the covariates

Two models rather than one is not redundancy. If Chronos beats climatology and TimesFM does not, the finding is about Chronos, not about forecasting gold — and that is a very different claim.

The result lives in 145 days, not 2,859. Both models were pretrained on corpora that close before 2026, and neither publishes an exact cutoff. Anything scored on 2015–2025 is partly measuring what the model memorised. The full history is reported because it is informative about the machinery; only the 2026 window can carry a claim.

What "done" looks like

A table of five RPS numbers at two horizons, on 145 unseen days, from a data path where nothing to the right of any cut-off was ever visible. If the models don't beat 0.625-flat climatology, that is the result — and it is worth knowing before building the other nine-tenths of the system on the assumption that they would.
